THE CLOCKMAKER'S MAP

Chapter One: The Old Workshop

At the end of a narrow street stood an old workshop with a blue wooden door. The sign above it read: Elias Finch, Clockmaker. Few people visited the shop anymore, but every morning at seven, Elias unlocked the door, opened the shutters, and listened to the quiet ticking of hundreds of clocks.

Elias had inherited the workshop from his grandfather. The room was crowded with wooden cabinets, brass gears, tiny springs, pendulums, and clocks of every shape. Some clocks were round, some square, and one was shaped like a small sailing ship.

One rainy afternoon, a young traveler named Mira entered the shop. She carried a leather bag and a folded piece of paper.

“I am looking for a clock,” she said.

Elias smiled. “That is a rather broad request.”

“It is a particular clock,” Mira replied. “My father owned it. He said that one day it would show me the way home.”

Elias studied the paper. It contained a rough drawing of a mountain, a river, and a strange symbol shaped like a crescent moon.

“I have seen this symbol before,” Elias said quietly.

Chapter Two: The Hidden Mechanism

Elias walked to the oldest cabinet in the workshop. He opened a drawer and removed a small brass key. Behind a row of dusty clocks was a wooden box.

Inside the box was a pocket watch.

The watch had no numbers on its face. Instead, it had twelve tiny stars. Its hands were perfectly still.

Mira picked it up.

The moment her fingers touched the watch, every clock in the workshop stopped ticking.

For several seconds, neither of them moved.

Then the pocket watch began to glow.

A thin line appeared across its glass, forming the shape of a map. The map showed a forest, a river, and a tower standing alone on a hill.

Elias looked toward the window.

“The map is pointing north,” he said.

Mira folded the paper and placed it inside her bag.

“Then I know where I have to go.”

Chapter Three: The Journey

The next morning, Mira left the town before sunrise. She followed the river north, carrying the pocket watch around her neck.

The road became smaller as the houses disappeared. Soon there were only trees, stones, and the sound of water moving between the rocks.

At sunset, she reached a wooden bridge.

On the other side stood an abandoned stone tower.

Mira held up the watch.

For the first time, its hands moved.

They pointed directly toward the tower.

She stepped onto the bridge.

Behind her, somewhere in the distance, a clock began to strike twelve.

Mira turned around, but there was nobody there.

She continued walking.

The tower door opened by itself.

Inside was a room filled with books, maps, and hundreds of photographs. On the largest table was a note with her name written on it.

Mira unfolded it carefully.

The first sentence read:

“If you have found this place, then the map has chosen you.”

She read the rest of the letter in silence.

At the bottom was a familiar signature.

Her father's name.

And beside it was a date from twenty years earlier.

The clock in her hand began ticking again.
